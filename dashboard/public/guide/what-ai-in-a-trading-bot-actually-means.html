What “AI” in a Trading Bot Actually Means

Most “AI trading bot” pages skip one fact: software can place trades for you, but it cannot know which way the price moves next. Here is what a Pocket Option bot really does, and how to test one before risking real money.

What the software actually does

Avalisa PO Bot is a Chrome extension. It does four jobs, inside the Pocket Option page in your own browser, on your own account:

  1. Decides when and which side to trade. In Avalisa Bot mode the engine is a fixed set of four chart rules (RSI 14, a 20-period moving average, Bollinger Bands, and momentum/candle checks). It trades only when enough of them agree: Low, Mid and High intensity require 2, 3 or all 4 rules. That is rule-based automation, not a learning machine — the word “AI” in this product category is mostly marketing.
  2. Sizes each stake. Martingale mode multiplies the next stake after a loss, by 2x up to 3x depending on your setting. Martingale itself reads no indicators.
  3. Applies your filters. Trade expiry, a minimum payout percentage, and which pairs to scan.
  4. Clicks for you. Trades are placed in your own Pocket Option tab. Keep that tab visible — a background or minimised tab can delay trade-result detection.

Rules, money management and execution. There is no crystal ball in the box.

What no bot can predict

  • The next candle. No software knows the direction in advance. Claims of near-perfect direction-reading are sales talk.
  • Streaks. Markets produce losing runs, and losing runs are where Martingale gets expensive.

The number that matters is break-even. Binary options pay a fixed percentage p, so the break-even win rate is 1 / (1 + p). At a 92% payout that is about 52.1%; at 80% it is about 55.6%. Our own measurements (September 2026) found no setting that beat break-even over large samples; Martingale measured around 43–44%. We publish that because you should judge any bot, ours included, against this bar. More in honest win rates and break-even.

Why Martingale needs a cap

Multiplying the stake after each loss means a losing streak compounds. Steps = N allows N + 1 trades in a losing cycle; at 2x, Steps 3 costs 15 times the base stake. Limiting Steps is the real way to cap a losing streak — there is no separate stop-loss or daily-loss-limit setting. When the next stake would exceed your balance, the bot pauses. See Martingale risk explained.

How to test it before real money

  1. Start on a demo account. The extension places trades the same way on demo and real accounts, so demo is the right first test.
  2. Mind the payout. A lower payout raises your break-even win rate before you trade once. Use the minimum-payout filter — see why the payout filter matters.
  3. If you use Martingale, set Steps to a small finite number.
  4. Judge over many trades, not one day. Compare your result with your break-even number.

Risk statement

Trading binary options is high risk; only trade money you can afford to lose. Martingale doubles (or more) the stake after each loss, and a losing streak can wipe out a balance. Trading involves risk. Avalisa does not guarantee profits. Start on a demo account.

Frequently asked questions

Does Avalisa use AI or machine learning?

No. Avalisa Bot mode is a fixed rule engine (2, 3 or 4 of 4 chart rules), and Martingale mode is stake sizing. Neither learns or predicts.

What is the difference between the two modes?

Martingale mode is money management and is on every plan (10 trades on the free Demo). Avalisa Bot mode is the rule-based signal engine and needs Pro.
