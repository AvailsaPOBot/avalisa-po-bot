First: what a bot actually is

A browser-based Pocket Option bot works inside your own logged-in browser session: it reads the Pocket Option page and places trades according to rules you set. It does not need to move your money anywhere. If a “bot” asks for your Pocket Option password, asks you to send it funds, or charges for “winning signals”, slow down and read on.

1. Check the publisher, not just the name

Look at who published the extension or app. A real publisher has a name you can search, a website and a support address. On the Chrome Web Store, check the listing itself: publisher, reviews and last update date.

2. Read the permissions it asks for

Open the extension's details in Chrome and read the permissions list before you install. A bot for one website should mainly need that website. If it asks for broad access you cannot explain — every site you visit, your downloads, your browsing history — ask the publisher why.

3. Insist on a demo account first

Pocket Option offers a demo account with virtual money. Run any bot there first, for long enough to see a losing streak, and only then decide. If a seller pressures you to “go real” quickly, that pressure is the red flag. The demo period is where you find out what the bot actually does on your pairs and timeframes.

4. Find out exactly what happens after a losing trade

Many Pocket Option bots use Martingale: after a loss, the next stake is multiplied. That can recover a short losing streak — and it multiplies your exposure when the streak runs long. Ask three questions: what is the multiplier, how many recovery steps are allowed, and what is the total stake across a full losing cycle?

At 2x, a cycle of 5 trades costs 31 times the base stake; at 3x it costs 121 times. A losing streak can wipe out a balance. Only trade money you can afford to lose. Our Martingale risk page has the full table.

5. Treat any fixed win rate as a warning sign

Results vary by pair, payout and timeframe, and markets change. A product that advertises a fixed success percentage or a monthly return is selling a number, not a tool. Remember the arithmetic too: at a 92% payout you need to win more than 52.1% of trades just to break even (break-even = 1 / (1 + payout)). We explain that in win rate, payout and break-even.

6. Check the operational reality: it must see the screen

A browser-extension bot reads trade results from the tab where Pocket Option is open. A hidden or minimised tab can delay result detection — and for a Martingale bot, the next stake depends on the last result. A trustworthy product tells you its browser requirements plainly.

7. Check how you were referred to it

Affiliate links and signal-group funnels are common. They are not automatically scams, but they change incentives: the person who sent you may be paid when you register or deposit, whatever happens to your trading. Before buying anything, go to the publisher's own website and store listing rather than relying only on the link you were sent.

8. Check the money path end to end

Before paying: what exactly are you buying, is it one-time or a recurring subscription, and is there a support address you can write to first? Keep a record of every payment and every message. Your deposits and withdrawals should go only through Pocket Option's own pages — never through the bot seller.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to run a Pocket Option bot in Chrome?

A browser extension is software you install, so the same care applies as for any extension: check the publisher and the listing, read the permissions, and never type your password into anything other than Pocket Option's own site. Trading risk is a separate question — that comes from the market and the stake plan, not from the extension.

Can any bot remove the risk from trading?

No. Binary options are high risk and results vary by pair, payout and timeframe. Anyone promising fixed results is selling words, not a tool.

Does Pocket Option allow bots?

Pocket Option's own blog says automated strategies are fine as long as they comply with platform rules, and names market manipulation — not automation — as the explicit ban risk. That is their blog, not their formal Terms of Service, which we have not read directly; for a definitive answer on your own account, ask Pocket Option support. The companion question — is there an official bot at all? — is covered in is there an official Pocket Option bot.

Should I use Martingale?

Decide only after you know the multiplier and step cap, have worked out the full-cycle cost, and have watched a losing streak on demo.

What if the bot misses a trade result?

Keep the Pocket Option tab visible while the bot trades. If results arrive late, the fix is operational — a visible tab and an active session — never a bigger stake.
