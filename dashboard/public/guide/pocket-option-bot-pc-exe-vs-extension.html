What a downloaded .exe can reach that a browser extension cannot

A browser extension runs inside one browser and, at most, one site's page. A program you download and install runs as its own process on your whole computer: it can open its own windows, read and write files, run in the background after you close its window, and reach things outside any single tab. That wider reach is why you should know exactly what you are installing and how to remove it later.

How it gets into your trading account

Many desktop bots ask you to type your Pocket Option login into the program itself, and then store it — in a config file or inside the program's own data — so it can sign in on its own. Your credentials then live on your computer outside your browser. A browser extension does not need your stored login: it works on the tab where you are already signed in, so the account session stays in your browser as usual.

Updates and removal

A downloaded program usually arrives as an installer, may start background processes, and leaves files and settings behind after you close it. Removing it means running the uninstaller (or removing it from Windows' installed-apps list) and checking that no background process keeps running. With a browser extension, removal is the Remove button in your browser's extensions list — no uninstaller and nothing left running outside the browser. Frequently asked questions

Do I have to install anything to use a Pocket Option bot?

Not a separate program. A browser extension like Avalisa is added to Chrome from the Chrome Web Store and runs on your signed-in Pocket Option tab — nothing to install outside the browser.

Is it safer to keep my login in my browser than in a downloaded program?

A downloaded bot that stores your login keeps your credentials on your computer outside your browser, where you have less control over them. An extension that works on your already-signed-in tab does not need to store your login at all.
