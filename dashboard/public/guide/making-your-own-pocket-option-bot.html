The six parts any bot needs

  1. Reading the page. The live pair, current payout, expiry countdown and each finished trade’s result. This touches the platform’s page directly, so it is the part most likely to stop working silently.
  2. A written entry rule. If you cannot state it in one sentence, you cannot test it.
  3. Stake sizing. How much per trade and how it changes after a loss. With Martingale, a losing streak can wipe out a balance, so capping the steps is how you cap the streak.
  4. Placing the trade. The right button, amount and moment, without double-firing.
  5. Detecting the result. Fast enough to run the next step of the rule; a late reading corrupts the next stake.
  6. Stopping. A hard rule for when the bot stops — a step cap, a balance check, a session end.

Each part is easy to describe and hard to make reliable, because each depends on the one before it.

What breaks when the page changes

A browser bot has no formal contract with the site; it reads and clicks the page as it looks today. When a label moves or the trade panel is restyled, reading and clicking can stop with no error at all. That is the maintenance cost nobody prices in — and any bot, yours or someone else’s, needs re-checking after platform updates. The tab matters too: a background or minimised tab can delay result detection, so keep it visible while the bot runs.

Why a long demo sample is the real work

At payout p, break-even is 1 / (1 + p) — 52.1% at a 92% payout. A rule that wins half its trades still loses money over time, and a short run cannot tell you which side of 52.1% your rule sits on. Run it on a demo account for a few hundred trades before trusting it. See testing a bot on demo and the break-even maths.

When an existing extension makes more sense

If six parts plus maintenance is more than you want to own, a finished extension handles them as one maintained system. Avalisa PO Bot is a Chrome extension for Pocket Option with a Martingale mode and a rule-based Avalisa Bot mode (Pro), on Demo and Real accounts. In Martingale mode you cap a streak by limiting Steps — there is no separate stop-loss setting — and the bot pauses when the next stake would exceed the balance.

The honest trade-off: building gives you full control and costs you the maintenance; using an existing tool means trusting someone else’s. Either way, break-even does not move.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make a Pocket Option bot without code?

Not really. Reading the page, placing trades and detecting results all need software. If you are weighing writing one yourself, first run the GitHub checklist for judging existing bots. What you can avoid is writing and maintaining it yourself.

How do I know my rule works before funding an account?

Run it on demo long enough for the sample to mean something. At a 92% payout break-even is 52.1%, so short runs cannot separate a losing rule from luck.

Will Pocket Option ban me for using a bot?

Pocket Option’s own blog says automated strategies are fine as long as they comply with platform rules, and names market manipulation — not automation itself — as the suspension risk. That is their stated position, not their formal Terms of Service, so read those before relying on it.
