The short answer

Trading bots are a normal part of how many Pocket Option traders work — the question most people should ask first is not “is a bot allowed” but “does my account stay safe, and do I know exactly what the bot will do before real money is on the line?” That second question is answerable, and the answer is: start on a demo account, cap your risk per trade, and only consider real money once you understand the behavior fully.

What the platform's rules actually say

Pocket Option's own published content (not their formal Terms of Service, which we have not read line by line) says users “can utilize automated strategies as long as they comply with platform rules.” It does not name third-party browser extensions specifically — it neither approves nor bans them by name. The one concrete risk their content does name is for market manipulation: creating false trading volumes or exploiting platform vulnerabilities. That is a different thing from a browser extension that clicks the same buttons you would click yourself, at the amounts and timing you configure.

If you want the definitive answer for your own account, Pocket Option's support team or their full Terms and Conditions page is the authoritative source — we're telling you what we found, not speaking for them. For that question specifically, see whether Pocket Option has an official bot.

What we can state from our own verified experience:

  • A browser extension trades through the same Pocket Option web interface you use, in the same logged-in session, in your own browser. It is your account and your choices, not someone else's account trading for you.
  • The bot works on both Demo and Real Pocket Option accounts. That is why we can — and do — recommend demo-first: you can observe the exact same behavior before a single real unit is at stake.
  • Avalisa PO Bot is publicly listed on the Chrome Web Store, which means anyone can inspect the listing, its permissions, and its ratings before installing.

How the bot works, in plain terms

The extension sits on top of your open Pocket Option tab and places trades according to settings you choose:

  • Strategy: Martingale, or Avalisa Bot mode (a rule-based signal mode, available on the Pro plan).
  • Direction (Martingale): Always Buy, Always Sell, or Alternating.
  • Timeframe and payout filters: you choose the trade expiry and a minimum payout percentage, so you decide in advance what kinds of trades the bot may take.
  • Stake multiplier: after a loss, the stake increases by a fixed multiplier you select (2x through 3x).
  • Martingale steps: a number, or infinite. If the next stake would exceed your balance, the bot pauses instead of placing it.

Two limitations worth knowing up front: the bot does not have a separate stop-loss or daily-loss-limit setting (limiting the number of Martingale steps is how you cap a losing streak), and it detects trade results through the visible tab — a backgrounded or minimized tab can delay result detection. Keep the Pocket Option tab visible while the bot is running.

Account safety: practical steps

  1. Start on a demo account. Every recommended configuration should be watched on demo first. The bot behaves identically on demo and real accounts, so nothing is skipped by waiting.
  2. Cap the Martingale steps. Martingale doubles (or multiplies) the stake after each loss, and a long losing streak can wipe out a balance. A step limit is the built-in way to stop that. There is no hidden daily-loss limit doing it for you — you set the cap.
  3. Set a minimum payout percentage. Low-payout trades need more right trades to cover losses; the payout filter lets you exclude them before you start.
  4. Only trade money you can afford to lose. Binary options are high risk; a losing sequence is ordinary, not exceptional. If a loss would hurt your monthly budget, the size is wrong.
  5. Keep credentials to yourself. The bot runs inside your own browser session. Nothing in normal use requires giving anyone else your login, moving money out of your account, or disabling your broker's own protections.
  6. Understand what you installed. Check the extension's Chrome Web Store listing and permissions before install. If a request ever looks beyond “operate on your open Pocket Option tab,” that is information you should bring to support, not something to shrug at.

The risk statement, plainly

Trading binary options is high risk and most retail traders lose money over time; binary-option expiry trades are all-or-nothing by design. Avalisa PO Bot does not guarantee profit — and we will not let any page or message imply otherwise. Our own measurements have found no setting that beats break-even over large samples, so treat any setting you read about, including ones we publish, as a hypothesis to test on demo, not a promise. The bot's value is discipline and consistency — executing one configured strategy without emotional deviation — not a source of sure income.

Frequently asked questions

Does Pocket Option officially permit third-party trading tools?

Pocket Option's own published content says automated strategies are allowed “as long as they comply with platform rules,” without naming browser extensions specifically, and it flags market manipulation — not automation itself — as the concrete ban risk. That's not their full Terms of Service, so for a definitive answer on your account, ask their support directly. What we can tell you: our bot runs inside your own browser and account, works on both demo and real accounts, and is distributed through the standard Chrome Web Store.

Will the bot trade while my tab is minimized?

It can, but result detection can be delayed in a backgrounded or minimized tab. Keep the Pocket Option tab visible while the bot is active.

What stops a losing streak from draining the account?

The Martingale step limit — when the next stake would exceed your balance, the bot pauses. Choose a step count that fits a budget you can afford to lose, and test it on demo before real money.

Does the extension promise trading results?

No. It does not guarantee profit, and no setting should be believed that way. Demo testing, step caps, and payout filters are how you keep your own exposure bounded.

How do I check whether my Pocket Option ID has a plan?

The setup guide at avalisabot.vercel.app includes a “Check my Pocket Option ID” lookup, so anyone can verify plan status directly.
