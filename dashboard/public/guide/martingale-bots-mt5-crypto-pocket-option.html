What a Martingale bot does on any platform

A Martingale bot follows one rule in every market: after a losing trade it raises the next stake (typically doubling it), and after a winning trade it resets to the starting amount. On MT5, on a crypto exchange or on a binary-options platform the loop is the same. What differs is what a win pays back, and whether that payout is fixed in advance.

How a fixed payout changes the maths

When the payout is below 100%, a win after a losing streak does not recover a full base stake. At a 92% payout with a 2x multiplier: lose 1, lose 2, then win on a stake of 4, which pays 4 × 0.92 = 3.68. Losses were 1 + 2 = 3, so the cycle nets +0.68 of a base stake, not +1.

Because the payout is fixed, the cost of a full losing cycle is a fixed multiple of the base stake, known before you trade. In Avalisa’s panel, Steps = N allows N + 1 trades in a losing cycle:

Variable-outcome markets: where the fixed table stops applying

On MT5, forex or a crypto exchange, the size of a win depends on how far price moved, so there is no fixed payout and no cycle-cost multiple to look up beforehand. The doubling still compounds losses the same way — a long losing streak still consumes a geometric series of stakes — but each win’s recovery varies trade by trade, so the table above does not transfer. Fixed payouts also give a fixed break-even win rate of 1 / (1 + p): 52.1% at a 92% payout. Variable markets need their own position sizing and risk limits.

Frequently asked questions

Does the cycle-cost table apply to my MT5 or crypto bot?

No. It applies where the payout per trade is fixed in advance, as on a fixed-payout binary-options platform. Variable-outcome markets have no fixed multiple to check.

Is there a stop-loss setting on Avalisa?

No. Limiting Martingale Steps is the only cap on a losing streak; there is no separate stop-loss or daily-loss-limit setting.

Can I test a capped setup before using real money?

Yes. Avalisa runs on Pocket Option Demo accounts as well as Real ones, with the same trade logic and the same Steps cap.
