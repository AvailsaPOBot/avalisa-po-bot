What a signal actually is

Strip away the marketing and a Pocket Option signal is three fields:

  • Direction — Buy (up) or Sell (down).
  • Asset — which pair or instrument the trade is on.
  • Expiry — how long the trade runs before it settles.

Screenshots, confidence percentages and indicator readouts are context for those three fields. If a source cannot be reduced to direction, asset and expiry, it is commentary, not a tradeable signal.

Three ways a signal reaches a trade

  1. Manual copy. You read the signal and place the trade yourself. The expiry clock is already running while you click.
  2. Telegram relay. A bot posts signals into a channel and you copy them. Faster than reading a post, but you are still the execution layer — and you can miscopy the asset, amount or direction.
  3. In-browser rules. An extension reads the chart in your own Pocket Option tab and applies written rules there, so the decision and the trade happen in one place with no copy step. Avalisa PO Bot’s Avalisa Bot strategy works this way.

Why “prediction” is the wrong word

At payout p, a binary option breaks even at a win rate of 1 / (1 + p). At a 92% payout that is 52.1%, not 50%, because a losing trade loses the whole stake.

“Prediction” implies clearing that line consistently. Our own large-sample measurements found no setting that beat break-even (Martingale measured about 43–44%). So the honest question for any signal source is not “how good are its predictions?” but “what win rate did it record against 1/(1+p), over how many trades, and can I see the record?” The derivation is on the break-even page.

How the Avalisa Bot strategy forms its own entry

Avalisa Bot (a Pro feature) does not wait for anyone’s signal. It checks four written rules against the chart and enters only when enough agree, set by Intensity:

  • Low — at least 2 of 4 rules agree.
  • Mid — at least 3 of 4.
  • High — all 4.

Intensity is a strictness dial: High means fewer entries with more agreement, Low means more entries with less. It changes how often the bot trades, not the break-even maths above.

What to check before trusting any signal source

  • The record, not screenshots. A timestamped trade history, and its win rate against 1/(1+p) for the payout actually paid.
  • The cost of being late. How old is a typical signal by the time you could realistically place it?
  • Who executes. With manual copy or a relay, you do — including the mistakes.
  • What happens after a loss. If the source says to double up, that is Martingale: at 2x, two straight losses cost 3× the base stake, three cost 7×, four 15×, five 31×. Cap it — see Martingale risk explained.

Frequently asked questions

Is a “prediction bot” just a signal bot with better marketing?

Usually. The output is the same three fields. What differs is who decides — a person, a relay or rules in your browser. Judge the decision process and the measured record, not the label. If the name you are weighing is Lyra, Neuro or Gemini, see the named-bots guide.

Does automation make signals more profitable?

No. Faster entry does not move break-even. A faster pipe into an unmeasured strategy is still an unmeasured strategy.

Can I try this without risking money?

Yes. Use a Pocket Option demo account. The extension places trades the same way on demo and real accounts, so demo shows whether the mechanics and settings do what you expect — not that the market will pay later. See testing on demo.
