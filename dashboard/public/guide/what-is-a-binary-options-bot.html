A binary option in one paragraph

A binary option is a fixed-payout bet on whether a price ends above or below its current level at a set expiry. You pick a direction and a stake. If you are right you receive a fixed payout p on the stake (at 92%, a $10 stake returns $19.20); if you are wrong you lose the whole stake. Because a loss costs more than a win pays, the break-even win rate is 1 / (1 + p) — at a 92% payout that is 52.1%, not 50%. The derivation is on the break-even page.

The three kinds of “bot” people mean

  1. Signal relays. A channel or website tells you when and which way to trade. You still place every trade yourself, so timing and discipline stay with you.
  2. Scripts and APIs. Standalone programs that place trades through code. They need a developer setup, usually your credentials stored in the program, and ongoing maintenance.
  3. In-browser extensions. Software inside your own browser that reads the trading page you already have open and places trades through the broker’s own interface, in your own session.

Avalisa PO Bot is the third kind: a Google Chrome extension that automates trades on Pocket Option in your own browser. It is not a signal service.

What any bot controls — and what none controls

Any bot controls the inputs of a trade: the stake, the direction rule, the expiry, and when to stop. That is the whole list.

No bot controls the next candle. It cannot make price move its way or know how the next expiry will settle. Its real value is discipline: applying one written rule the same way every time and stopping where you told it to. A listing that says a bot “predicts” the market is claiming the one thing no bot controls.

If a bot offers Martingale (raising the stake after a loss), check how it counts steps. In Avalisa’s panel, Steps = N allows N + 1 trades in a losing cycle: at 2x, Steps 2 is $1 → $2 → $4, 7× the base stake. A losing streak can wipe out a balance — see Martingale risk explained.

Questions to ask before using any bot

  • What exactly does it control? Stake, direction rule, expiry, stopping. Anything beyond that is a marketing claim.
  • Where do my credentials go? A script may need them stored in the program; an in-browser extension works in the session you are already logged into.
  • How does it stop? A bot with no limit keeps going until the balance decides for it.
  • Can I test it on demo first? If not, you are testing with real money.
  • What does it cost? Compare plans before paying — see how to choose a Pocket Option bot.

Frequently asked questions

Is a binary options bot the same as a signal bot?

No. A signal bot tells you what to trade and you place it by hand; a trading bot places the trade itself. Avalisa places trades, in your browser, on Pocket Option.

Can a bot make me win more often?

No bot can promise that. It applies a fixed rule and your stopping limits. Break-even, 1/(1+p), is set by the payout, not by the bot.
