Candle Streak and Pin-Bar Rules: What They Assume

“Candle streak” and “pin bar” are popular entry signals in Pocket Option automation talk. Both are easy to code — the real question is whether the assumption underneath each signal holds. Up front: Avalisa does not trade candle streaks or pin bars and has no streak or pin-bar setting. This page explains them for education.

What the two signals are

  • Candle streak (colour streak): after N consecutive candles in the same direction, the next trade goes either with the streak (continuation) or against it (reversal). You pick N and the rule; the rest is mechanical.
  • Pin bar: a candle with a small body and a long wick. A long lower wick is read as “the market rejected lower prices”, a long upper wick as rejection of higher prices.

Assumption 1: recent direction predicts the next candle

A streak rule assumes momentum or exhaustion — that five green candles make a sixth more or less likely. That is a statistical claim about one pair, one timeframe and the conditions of the minute you trade it. It does not carry over automatically from chart to chart or hour to hour.

The number that matters on Pocket Option: at a 92% payout, break-even needs a 52.1% correct-direction rate. In our large-sample measurements no setting beat that — Martingale measured roughly 43–44%. Honest win rates and break-even explains the line. Treat “the streak works” as a claim to test on demo, not a property of the pattern.

Assumption 2: a candle shape predicts the expiry

Pin-bar reading comes from markets where a rejected wick may say something about nearby price action. A binary option asks something narrower: is price on your side of entry at the chosen expiry? Two identical-looking pin bars entered seconds apart can end differently purely from where entry sat.

The payout sets the line any pattern has to clear: the break-even win rate is 1 / (1 + p), so about 52.1% at a 92% payout, and higher still at lower payouts. That is why a minimum-payout filter and a sensible timeframe matter at least as much as the pattern.

Assumption 3: the recovery strategy funds the losing streak

Most streak traders pair the signal with Martingale — multiply the stake after each loss so one win recovers the run. Martingale maths does not care about the entry signal. In the Avalisa panel, Steps = N allows N + 1 trades in a losing cycle:

  • 2x, Steps 5: 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + 16 + 32 = 63× the base stake.
  • 3x, Steps 5: 1 + 3 + 9 + 27 + 81 + 243 = 364× the base stake.
  • At a 2% base stake, a full 3x Steps 5 cycle is 364 × 2% = 728% of the balance — more than the whole account.

The signal decides how often you enter a cycle; the multiplier and Steps decide what each one costs. Work out your own numbers with the Martingale calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Does a longer streak make the signal stronger?

Not automatically. A longer streak is a stricter filter, so you get fewer trades — fewer is not the same as better. Only demo history on your own pair and timeframe can answer it.

Is Martingale the same thing as a streak rule?

No. A streak rule chooses when and which way to enter; Martingale decides the stake after each loss. Either way the multiplier sets the cycle cost, so keep Steps capped.

Why demo first?

The extension places trades the same way on demo and real accounts, so demo history is a fair test of your settings before money is at stake.
