What Pocket Option actually says

Pocket Option's blog has a post answering whether trading bots are legal. Its position: you can use automated strategies as long as they comply with platform rules.

The one explicit suspension or ban risk their own content names is market manipulation — creating false trading volumes or exploiting platform vulnerabilities. That is about what you do, not about whether a person or a program clicks the button.

Some other websites (not Pocket Option itself) describe Pocket Option as offering a developer API and being compatible with third-party bots, without endorsing any specific one. That fits the blog position, but it is still not Pocket Option's formal terms.

What we do not know — said plainly

We have not read Pocket Option's formal Terms of Service directly, and the blog post does not mention browser extensions by name either way. So treat “allowed” as the broker's stated position, not as a protected right. For a definitive answer about your own account, ask Pocket Option support before you trade real money.

What Avalisa PO Bot actually does

Avalisa PO Bot is a Google Chrome extension that places trades on Pocket Option in your own browser, on your own account, according to the settings you choose. It works on Demo and Real accounts and places trades the same way on both.

Deposits and withdrawals stay inside Pocket Option's own normal flow — they do not depend on whether a trade was placed by hand or by the extension.

How to keep your risk small

  1. Start on a demo account. Run the exact settings you plan to use on Pocket Option's demo account until you have watched a losing streak and understand what the bot does.
  2. Read Pocket Option's rules yourself in your account area before moving to a real account, and ask their support if anything is unclear. If their text is stricter than this page, their text wins.
  3. Never do anything that looks like manipulation — that is the risk the broker names.
  4. Cap Martingale. Martingale multiplies the stake after each loss, and a losing streak can wipe out a balance. In the panel, Martingale Steps = N allows N + 1 trades in a losing cycle; at 2x, Steps 4 means a full losing cycle costs 31 times the base stake. There is no separate stop-loss setting — limiting Steps is the cap. See Martingale risk explained.
  5. Keep the Pocket Option tab visible while the bot trades. A background or minimised tab can delay trade-result detection.
  6. Only trade money you can afford to lose. A bot changes who presses the button, not the odds.

Is there an automatic “bot ban”?

We have not seen any Pocket Option statement describing one. Their published content names market manipulation as the explicit risk. We cannot speak for Pocket Option's own systems.

If I lose money using the bot, is anyone responsible?

You are trading on your own account. The bot does not guarantee profit and does not promise any win rate. At a 92% payout you need to win more than 52.1% of trades just to break even (break-even = 1 / (1 + payout)) — see win rate, payout and break-even.

Can I still withdraw if I used a bot?

Withdrawals go through Pocket Option's own normal flow, the same as for manual trades. More in can you withdraw funds after bot trading.
