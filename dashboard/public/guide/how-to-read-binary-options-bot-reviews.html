Why screenshots and short streaks prove nothing

  • Break-even maths. At a payout p, you need a win rate above 1 / (1 + p) just to break even — 52.1% at a 92% payout, 55.6% at 80%. A streak of wins says nothing about whether a bot clears that line. See honest win rates and break-even.
  • Sample size. Ten, twenty or fifty trades cannot separate an edge from luck. A result with no total trade count is unmeasured.

Demo screenshots are weaker still, for the reasons in why bots win on demo and lose on real.

Signs a review is useful

  • Total trade count, not just a final balance.
  • The exact settings (timeframe, stake, multiplier, steps), so you could repeat the test.
  • Demo or real account, stated plainly.
  • Losses shown, including losing streaks. A record that only goes up is a curated record.

Signs it is not

  • Income claims — “$X to $Y”, monthly income, win-rate promises.
  • Referral or affiliate links with no disclosure that the poster earns a commission.
  • No losses anywhere in the screenshots or numbers.
  • Urgency: “limited spots”, “price goes up tonight”.

You do not need to accuse anyone. Just give the claim less weight and check the numbers yourself.

Run your own small demo test instead

  1. Write your settings down first: timeframe, stake, multiplier, steps.
  2. Run on a demo account and record every result, wins and losses.
  3. Compare your win rate to 1 / (1 + p) for your payout. Below break-even means the settings do not work.
  4. If you test Martingale, know the cycle cost. In Avalisa’s panel, Steps = N allows N + 1 trades in a losing cycle: at 2x, Steps 2 costs 7× the base stake and Steps 3 costs 15×. See Martingale risk explained.
  5. Read the total, not the best moment. A small test can show a setting is clearly broken; only hundreds of trades say much more, and no test promises future results. See how to test a trading bot on demo.

Frequently asked questions

A Reddit user posted weeks of green screenshots. Should I believe them?

Screenshots show a chosen slice. Without the total trade count, the settings and the losing streaks, treat the result as unmeasured.

The review links to a checkout page. Does that disqualify it?

Not by itself, but an undisclosed referral link means the reviewer may earn a commission. Named products deserve named scrutiny: see the named-bots guide (Lyra, Neuro, Gemini). Weigh the numbers accordingly and verify on demo.

How many demo trades do I need?

Fifty is enough to catch a clearly broken setting; judging a win rate close to break-even takes hundreds. Short runs sit inside normal luck.
