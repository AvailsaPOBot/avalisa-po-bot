What the offers in search results claim

The offers tend to come in a few recurring shapes:

  • A Telegram account or channel that “sends signals” or “trades for you” once you message or join.
  • An APK file (an Android app) offered as a “free download” from a website rather than an app-store listing you can verify.
  • A “free bot” unlocked after you open a new broker account through their link or reply with your details.
  • Profit screenshots attached to the offer itself.

None of these formats is automatically a scam. What they share is that you hand something over — account details, a new account, money — before you can see how the thing works. Buying directly from an individual seller is its own checklist: see buying a bot from a seller.

For a side-by-side comparison of the two product types, read browser extension vs Telegram bots.

How such a bot would have to reach your account

A program cannot trade on your Pocket Option account from nowhere. A third-party bot needs one of these:

  • Your login details. You send a username and password so their software signs in as you. Whoever holds them can see and act in your account.
  • A program on your device. You install their APK or script and trust it with everything it can reach on that device.
  • A new account through their link. The account is tied to them from day one.

Each is a normal mechanism. The question is whether you know exactly what access you are granting, to whom, and how you take it back.

Warning signs worth pausing on

  • Profit screenshots. A screenshot shows one session chosen by whoever took it. It says nothing about the losing days.
  • An APK as the delivery method. Check where it comes from and what permissions it asks for — give it the scrutiny you would give anything near your bank app.
  • Login first. If you must send credentials before you see the bot work, you have granted full access before any trust.
  • A new account is required. You cannot compare the bot against anything you already know.
  • Urgency. “Free today only”, limited slots, countdowns. Real software can be evaluated calmly.

What to check before you pay or send anything

  1. Can I see it work before I pay? A demo account or a visible session beats a screenshot.
  2. What exactly am I granting — credentials, an installed program, a referred account — and how do I take it back?
  3. Where did the software come from, and can I verify that source myself?
  4. What is the cost: one-time, subscription, or “free until you need it”?
  5. Have I tried it on a demo account first? See how to test a bot on demo.

Frequently asked questions

Do “free” Telegram bots cost anything?

Often they cost access rather than money: your login details or a new account opened through their link. Judge the offer on that price.

Is an APK automatically unsafe?

No, but an APK from a search result, outside a store listing you can verify, deserves extra scrutiny before it goes near a device that holds your trading account.
