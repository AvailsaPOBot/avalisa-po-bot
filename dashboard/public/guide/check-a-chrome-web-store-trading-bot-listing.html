What a listing tells you

A Chrome Web Store listing is the closest thing an extension has to a product label. Before you click Install, look for:

  • Publisher — a named publisher, not an anonymous account.
  • Permissions — the browser-wide powers the extension asks for.
  • Site access — which websites it can read and change.
  • Reviews — what people report after actually using it, and whether the publisher replies.
  • Last updated — a listing untouched for a long time may break when the trading site changes.
  • Support contact — an email or site where a person answers.

None of this replaces testing. A listing that passes every check still deserves a demo run first — see testing a bot on a demo account.

Reading permissions and site access

Permissions are browser-wide powers. storage, for example, only lets the extension save its own settings in your browser.

Site access (the manifest’s host_permissions) lists the websites the extension can read and modify. A Pocket Option panel needs Pocket Option’s own trading domains — that is where it reads the chart and places trades. It has no reason to reach your email, your bank or “all websites”.

As a worked example, this is everything Avalisa PO Bot’s manifest declares:

  • Permissions: storage — saves its own settings locally.
  • Site access: https://pocketoption.com/*, https://po.cash/*, https://po.trade/* — Pocket Option’s trading sites only.

That is the shape of a narrow, single-purpose extension. Compare any other listing against it.

Red flags in a listing

  • Access to all sites when it claims to do one job on one trading site.
  • No support contact — when money is on screen, you need a real address to write to.
  • Profit or win-rate promises — no honest listing guarantees results.
  • Asking for your broker password — a panel that works inside your already signed-in Pocket Option tab never needs it.

Frequently asked questions

Does a longer permissions list mean a better bot?

No. More access means more that can go wrong. For a Pocket Option panel, local settings storage plus access to the trading site is the expected shape.

The listing looks fine but reviews mention problems. Install anyway?

Read whether the problems are recent and whether the publisher answered. If they are common and unanswered, wait or choose another extension.

I installed the wrong one. What now?

Remove it from Chrome’s extensions page, and if it ever asked for your Pocket Option password, change that password. See how to stop or remove the bot safely.
