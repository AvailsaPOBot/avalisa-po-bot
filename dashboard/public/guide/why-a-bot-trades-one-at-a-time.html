Why a Trading Bot Should Hold One Trade at a Time

Avalisa PO Bot opens one trade, waits for its result, and only then decides the next one. That is slower than firing off parallel positions — and it is the point. Here is why sequential trading keeps the staking logic intact and a bad streak contained.

Each trade resolves on its own; Martingale stakes depend on sequence

A binary-options trade expires and resolves by itself. What ties two trades together is the staking plan, not the market. Martingale staking is deliberately conditional: after each loss the stake is multiplied — in Avalisa PO Bot the choices are 2x, 2.2x, 2.4x, 2.6x, 2.8x or 3x.

Because the next stake is only defined once the previous result is known, the loop has to be sequential: open one trade, wait for its result, then decide the next. Avalisa PO Bot works this way — while a trade is open it does not place another. “One trade at a time” is not a limitation of this kind of bot; it is the shape the staking maths requires.

Why overlapping trades are worse, not faster

  • Exposure compounds. A single Martingale sequence can already wipe out a balance during a losing streak. Running several at once multiplies the money at risk without adding any new information.
  • The stake stops being defined. If several positions resolve together, “what is my stake after that loss?” becomes ambiguous. With at most one live position, there is no ambiguity.
  • Results stay attributable. The extension reads results from the Pocket Option tab, and a background or minimised tab can delay result detection. One open trade keeps each result tied to one known position, so your history is reviewable.
  • The cap keeps working. There is no separate stop-loss or daily-loss-limit setting. Limiting Steps is the real way to cap a losing streak, and the bot pauses when the next stake would exceed your balance. Both assume one running sequence.

The maths to run before touching any setting

Binary options pay a fixed percentage p, so the break-even win rate is 1 / (1 + p). At a 92% payout that is about 52.1% — more than half of every trade, indefinitely. Our own measurements (September 2026) found no setting that beat break-even over large samples; Martingale measured around 43–44%. More in honest win rates and break-even.

And the cost of a streak: Steps = N allows N + 1 trades in a losing cycle; at 2x, Steps 3 costs 15 times the base stake. See Martingale risk explained.

Practical steps, in order

  1. Start on a demo account. The extension places trades the same way on demo and real accounts, so watch what a losing streak does there first.
  2. Set Martingale Steps to a small number, not Infinite. Work out the cycle cost with the rule above before you choose.
  3. Set a minimum payout. A lower payout raises your break-even rate — see why the payout filter matters.
  4. Choose pair behaviour deliberately. Pair Scan offers Current pair only, Auto scan favorites, or Auto-switch favorite. Fewer moving parts make results easier to read.
  5. Keep the Pocket Option tab visible while the bot runs.
  6. Change one setting at a time, and never mid-streak.

Risk statement

Trading binary options is high risk; only trade money you can afford to lose. Martingale multiplies the stake after each loss, and a losing streak can wipe out a balance. Trading involves risk. Avalisa does not guarantee profits. Start on a demo account.

Frequently asked questions

Is one trade at a time slower?

In trade count, yes. But each Martingale stake is defined by the previous result, so a sequential bot gives up nothing by waiting, while parallel trades add risk without adding information.

What happens if I lose several in a row?

The stake grows by your chosen multiplier after each loss, up to your Steps limit. When the next stake would exceed your balance, the bot pauses instead of continuing.

What is the difference between Martingale and Avalisa Bot mode?

Martingale follows a fixed direction (Always Buy, Always Sell or Alternating) and multiplies the stake after losses. Avalisa Bot mode needs Pro and trades only when enough of four chart rules agree: Low, Mid and High intensity require 2, 3 or all 4.
