What people mean by "official"

When someone asks for an "official Pocket Option bot", they usually mean one of three things:

  • A bot made by Pocket Option itself — something the broker builds, sells and supports directly.
  • A bot the broker recommends or endorses — a named third-party tool the broker points you to.
  • A built-in feature of the platform — an auto-trading switch inside the Pocket Option website.

The idea comes from a reasonable assumption: many platforms ship their own tools, and people assume that if a bot exists for a platform, the platform approved it. "A bot works with Pocket Option" and "Pocket Option stands behind this bot" are different claims. If a seller says their bot is official or endorsed, the only place that can confirm it is Pocket Option's own website or support — not the seller's page.

How to check who actually publishes a tool

Before you install or pay for any trading tool, find out who publishes it. It takes minutes:

  1. Developer name. On a Chrome Web Store listing the publisher name is shown on the page. Check that it matches the name on the product's own website.
  2. The store listing itself. Read the description, the permissions it requests and the reviews. Our walk-through: how to check a Chrome Web Store trading bot listing.
  3. The product's website. A real developer has documentation, pricing and a contact address. A listing with no traceable developer behind it is a red flag on its own.
  4. Permissions. A trading extension needs to work on your trading site's tab. If a listing asks for far more than that, ask why.

You should be able to name the company or person who made the tool you are about to install. If you cannot, you do not know who you are trusting.

Third-party tools vs broker features

  • Broker features are built into the platform by the broker: charts, trade buttons, account settings. You get them by using the platform; nobody sells them to you separately.
  • Third-party tools are built by someone else and added to your own browser or computer. A Chrome extension runs in your browser on your signed-in tab; it is not part of the broker's website.
  • A developer API, where a broker offers one, is an interface that lets third parties build tools. It does not make any particular bot "official".

A useful test: if the tool has its own price, its own developer name and its own install step outside the broker's website, it is a third-party product — however well it works with the platform. Compatibility is not endorsement.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an official Pocket Option bot I should use instead?

Every bot we know of for Pocket Option, including ours, is a third-party product you install yourself. If someone claims theirs is official, ask Pocket Option support to confirm it.

Does Pocket Option endorse a specific bot?

We have not seen Pocket Option name or endorse a specific third-party bot. Its own blog says automated strategies are fine as long as they comply with platform rules — that is their blog, not their formal Terms of Service. Treat "Pocket Option's official choice" as a marketing claim until you see it on the broker's own site.

How do I tell a broker feature from a third-party tool?

Ask who made it and where it is installed from. Its own developer name, its own price and its own install step mean third-party. Built into the broker's website and free with the account means a broker feature.
